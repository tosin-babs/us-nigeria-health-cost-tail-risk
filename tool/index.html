How heavy is each tail?

ξ is the shape of a generalised Pareto distribution fitted to the worst 10% of each group. Above zero the tail is heavy; above 0.5 the variance is infinite, meaning the next bad case can be far worse than the worst one seen. Every American group is above that line and no Nigerian group is close.

Why the shapes differ

The mechanism, and what this page cannot tell you

A Nigerian household facing a bill beyond its means does not pay it. It forgoes the care, sells something, or borrows within a narrow local limit. The observed burden is bounded by what could be raised, and the real loss shows up as untreated illness, which no spending statistic records. An American household in the same position is treated and then billed, and the bill can be financed. The observed burden is bounded by what can be borrowed, which is a far higher ceiling. The two distributions differ in shape because the two systems fail differently, and only one of those failures is visible in an out-of-pocket number.

The denominators are not the same variable. The American survey records income and no consumption; the Nigerian one records consumption and unreliable income. Each country uses its own standard measure, which is what WHO guidance allows, so this compares relative burden and not welfare. Income is more volatile than consumption, which if anything works against the finding rather than for it.

Neither survey sees forgone care. A household that went without treatment appears as a zero. Every figure here understates unmet need, and it understates it far more in Nigeria.

This is not a personal risk assessment. Placing your ratio on a distribution tells you where that ratio sits among households surveyed in 2019–2024 and 2023/24. It is not a prediction about you.
